\chapter{Theoretical Background}

\section{Introduction}

The design and implementation of ScholarPath AdDU is grounded in nine established theoretical frameworks drawn from computer science, information science, and software engineering. Together, these theories provide formal justification for every major architectural decision, from database structure and document management to security, user experience, and development methodology. This chapter situates each theory within the overarching Information Systems Success Model \cite{ref20} adopted in Chapter 2. Relational Database Theory and Metadata-Driven Document Management underpin System Quality by ensuring structural integrity and storage efficiency. Information Foraging Theory and Event-Driven Architecture support Information and Service Quality through improved discoverability and proactive communication. Rule-Based Expert Systems Theory operationalizes the automated eligibility matching engine. Finally, Cloud Infrastructure Theory, RBAC, Empirical Process Control, and the HCI/ISO 25010 framework collectively ensure scalability, legal compliance, iterative development, and empirically validated usability.

\section{Relational Database Theory and Normalization}

Consolidating student academic profiles, household financial metrics, and the eligibility criteria for the three internal scholarship tracks into a consistent repository requires a rigorous structural foundation. Relational Database Theory, specifically the normalization principles formalized by Codd \cite{ref17}, provides this foundation by mathematically organizing data into interconnected two-dimensional tables governed by first-order predicate logic. Within the DeLone and McLean \cite{ref20} framework, a normalized 3NF schema is the foundational enabler of System Quality (specifically its data integrity sub-dimension), as it eliminates the inconsistencies that would otherwise corrupt the automated matching engine's outputs.

\subsection{Progression Through Normal Forms}

The ScholarPath AdDU schema adheres to the Third Normal Form (3NF), progressing through Codd's sequential requirements. First Normal Form (1NF) mandates atomic, scalar values with no repeating groups; prerequisite documents such as Form 230-SCH and Income Tax Returns are therefore stored as discrete, queryable records rather than comma-separated strings. Second Normal Form (2NF) eliminates partial dependencies, ensuring grant provider details are stored independently of student application records. Third Normal Form (3NF) eliminates transitive dependencies, ensuring, as Kent \cite{ref32} summarizes, that every non-key attribute provides ``a fact about the key, the whole key, and nothing but the key.'' Adhering to 3NF guarantees that any update to a scholarship attribute, such as a revised QPI threshold or an extended deadline, propagates instantly from a single authoritative record to all related queries.

\section{Centralized Information Systems and Cloud Infrastructure Theory}

The decision to deploy ScholarPath AdDU as a single unified web platform, rather than distributing data across departmental servers, is grounded in centralized IT infrastructure theory \cite{ref34}. Centralized infrastructure consolidates computing resources and data into one managed environment, enabling uniform policy enforcement and the elimination of data silos that cause policy desynchronization across departments \cite{ref41}. By hosting all three internal scholarship tracks on a single Supabase backend, deadline updates and eligibility changes are immediately reflected for all users simultaneously. Modern cloud platforms mitigate the traditional risk of centralization, single points of failure, through hardware virtualization and physical redundancy. Mell and Grance \cite{ref34} define cloud computing as on-demand access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources characterized by rapid elasticity and measured service. For ScholarPath AdDU, Supabase's managed PostgreSQL infrastructure provides high availability alongside immutable audit logs that support compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), transforming regulatory requirements into an operational advantage. In the IS Success Model, centralized cloud infrastructure directly supports System Quality through 24/7 availability and Service Quality through uniform, institution-wide policy enforcement.

\section{Rule-Based Expert Systems Theory}

Automating the complex matching of students to eligible scholarships requires codifying the logic of the AdDU College Scholarship Committee as machine-executable rules. Rule-Based Expert Systems (RBS) theory provides the formal mechanism for this, representing human domain expertise as conditional IF-THEN logic \cite{ref15}. In the DeLone and McLean \cite{ref20} model, the matching engine operationalizes Information Quality, specifically its accuracy and completeness dimensions, by ensuring that only verifiably eligible grants are surfaced to the student's dashboard.

\subsection{Knowledge Representation and the Inference Engine}

Buchanan and Duda \cite{ref15} describe expert systems as consisting of three interdependent components: a Knowledge Base of facts and IF-THEN rules, a Working Memory storing the current input state, and an Inference Engine that applies rules to derive conclusions via the logical principle of modus ponens. In ScholarPath AdDU, the Knowledge Base is the normalized database of the three internal scholarship tracks with their eligibility constraints; the Working Memory is the student's profile at the time of a match request; and the Inference Engine applies a forward-chaining (data-driven) strategy, iterating from known student attributes (QPI, household income, and degree program) to an ordered array of eligible grants.

\subsection{Conflict Resolution and the Exclusion Flag Hierarchy}

A core design challenge in inference engines is conflict resolution: determining system behavior when multiple rules simultaneously apply to the same data state. ScholarPath AdDU implements a priority-based strategy called the Exclusion Flag Hierarchy, in which specific negative constraints unconditionally override general positive constraints. For example, a state-sponsored STEM grant may broadly include all students in the Science and Technology cluster, while a separate university policy excludes BS Nursing students due to non-coverable clinical fees. The inference engine resolves this by applying the exclusion flag and bypassing the grant entirely, regardless of whether the student satisfies QPI and income criteria. \section{Information Foraging Theory and Faceted Search}

The cognitive processes through which users navigate complex digital environments are formalized by Information Foraging Theory (IFT), developed by Pirolli and Card \cite{ref37} at PARC. IFT applies optimal foraging theory from evolutionary ecology to model how users maximize the rate of information gain while minimizing cognitive cost, analogous to how animals optimize food acquisition. IFT rests on three core constructs: Information Patches (localized clusters of related content within an interface), Information Diet (the specific subset of sources a user pursues to satisfy a goal), and Information Scent (the user's cognitive estimate of how likely a given path is to yield relevant results). When perceived scent drops below a threshold, users abandon the current patch and navigate elsewhere \cite{ref37}. In the AdDU scholarship context, presenting the three internal scholarship tracks as an unstructured list produces weak information scent and high abandonment. ScholarPath AdDU addresses this through Dynamic Faceted Search, which exposes the system's underlying metadata structure directly in the interface. Students intersect facets such as ``State-Sponsored Grants,'' ``Active Deadlines,'' or ``Full Tuition Coverage'' to iteratively refine the information patch and prevent empty result sets \cite{ref4,ref43}. Backend compound indexing and a 300-millisecond client-side debounce ensure instantaneous query response, preserving the low temporal cost critical to effective information foraging.

\section{Event-Driven Architecture for Asynchronous Notification}

The systemic problem of missed deadlines at AdDU is attributable to passive, manual communication channels. ScholarPath AdDU addresses this through Event-Driven Architecture (EDA), which shifts the notification system from a synchronous request-response model to an asynchronous, event-triggered one \cite{ref28}. EDA is built around the production, detection, and consumption of events (significant changes in system state) and operates on the principle of loose coupling, where components interact independently with no awareness of each other's internal logic. By adopting EDA, the notification subsystem operates without blocking the main application server. \section{Role-Based Access Control and Data Privacy}

Financial aid systems process highly sensitive personal and socioeconomic data, necessitating rigorous access control. ScholarPath AdDU implements the NIST Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) model, formalized by Ferraiolo and Kuhn \cite{ref24} and standardized by NIST in 2004. RBAC replaced error-prone, per-user Access Control Lists (ACLs) with a tripartite structure: Users are assigned Roles representing their organizational function, and Permissions are attached exclusively to those Roles \cite{ref38}. This structure inherently enforces two foundational security principles: the Principle of Least Privilege, which limits access to the minimum required for each user's duties, and Separation of Duties, which distributes critical workflows across roles to prevent unilateral fraud or error. RBAC is the direct theoretical mechanism by which ScholarPath AdDU achieves compliance with RA 10173. Because the Document Vault stores government-stamped ITRs and confidential household income data, a flat access model would constitute a legal liability. Applicants hold self-access only; Scholarship Staff hold global verification access; Interview Panel members are restricted to their assigned applicants; School Subcommittees are restricted to applicants within their school; the Scholarship Director holds global executive oversight; and the Office of Admission is limited to releasing endorsed lists (Table~\ref{tab:rbac}). All evaluator roles operate within one Unified Admin Dashboard: RBAC determines what each role can see and change within that interface, not which interface it uses. These boundaries are enforced at the database engine level through PostgreSQL Row Level Security (RLS) policies, ensuring that even if an application endpoint is accidentally exposed, the underlying data remains inaccessible without proper role-verified authentication. Within the IS Success Model, RBAC compliance is the mechanism by which the platform satisfies the Service Quality dimension, specifically the assurance sub-dimension, by guaranteeing that access to sensitive economic data is governed strictly and consistently.

\section{Empirical Process Control Theory in Agile Development}

The development of ScholarPath AdDU is governed by Empirical Process Control theory, the foundational pillar of Agile methodology \cite{ref39}. Unlike the Waterfall model, which assumes complete upfront specification of all requirements, empirical process control acknowledges that complex software systems operate in volatile, ambiguous environments where requirements emerge through iterative stakeholder interaction. The theory rests on three interdependent pillars \cite{ref39}: Transparency, requiring that all development progress and system artifacts be visible and understood by both developers and stakeholders; Inspection, requiring frequent examination of the system against standard metrics to detect variances; and Adaptation, requiring immediate process adjustment when inspection reveals unacceptable deviation. Because the three internal scholarship tracks contain undocumented edge cases that cannot be fully specified before development begins, a rigid sequential approach would produce a flawed matching engine. The Agile SDLC described in Chapter 3 operationalizes all three pillars: the functional prototype undergoes alpha testing and User Acceptance Testing (UAT) as the Inspection phase, and feedback from Scholarship Staff, subcommittee members, and applicant testers drives immediate schema adjustments and rule-logic refinements as the Adaptation phase. In the context of the IS Success Model, empirical process control supports System Quality by enabling continuous verification that the system's functional logic aligns with stakeholder requirements before each phase of testing. The continued relevance of this empirical approach in contemporary software development projects has been validated in recent literature. Hoda et al. \cite{ref27} conducted a systematic mapping of agile practices across 1,540 studies, confirming that transparency, iterative inspection, and stakeholder-driven adaptation remain the dominant predictors of project success in environments characterized by evolving or partially specified requirements, conditions that precisely describe the undocumented edge cases within AdDU's three-track financial aid taxonomy.

\section{Human-Computer Interaction and the ISO/IEC 25010 Model}

While robust backend architecture ensures computational reliability, the practical success of an information system is ultimately determined by user adoption. The interface design and formal evaluation of ScholarPath AdDU are therefore guided by Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) theory and the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 Software Quality Evaluation standard \cite{ref23,ref31}. HCI focuses on designing interactive computing systems that align with users' cognitive capacities and actual workflows \cite{ref23}. For AdDU students, the legacy process of physically tracking down documents, parsing eligibility criteria across multiple disparate websites, and submitting brown envelopes imposes unnecessary cognitive overhead. ScholarPath AdDU alleviates this through a centralized dashboard aggregating deadlines and eligibility statuses into a single view, and a mobile-responsive architecture enabling document uploads from any device, thereby bypassing the physical constraints of administrative office hours. The ISO/IEC 25010:2023 SQuaRE standard provides the formal evaluation framework, defining product quality across eight measurable characteristics.
