\section{Background of the Study}

Globally, universities are transitioning from traditional, paper-based administrative workflows to digital ecosystems to enhance student access to educational funding. This digitalization is increasingly necessary due to rising educational costs and expanding student populations. In the Philippines, the steady growth of the higher education sector \cite{ref29} has coincided with tuition fee increases across numerous private institutions, driven by inflation and operational adjustments \cite{ref11,ref19}. While financial aid is available through state-sponsored grants \cite{ref21,ref44}, international educational investments \cite{ref45}, and private corporate foundations \cite{ref1,ref40}, discovering and accessing these opportunities remains a challenge due to decentralized information.

At Ateneo de Davao University (AdDU), incoming applicants face significant administrative hurdles during the scholarship application process. For every application cycle, the University Scholarship Office handles about 1,326 paper applications across three internal tracks: Jubilee, Grant-in-Aid, and Working Scholars. The reliance on manual documentation creates severe paper overload at verification, requiring staff to process full physical files even for applicants who fail basic baseline requirements, such as high school average or entrance exam results. This creates operational bottlenecks and delays decision-making across the school subcommittees.

To address these challenges, this project proposes ScholarPath AdDU, a web-based scholarship application processing and committee decision-support system. The platform introduces a first-pass eligibility screening engine, the retargeted Smart Eligibility Checker, which evaluates an applicant's high school average, entrance exam score, and household income against baseline track requirements. It also incorporates a two-stage digital Document Vault supporting a 5-calendar-day Conditional Revert window to handle missing files and compliance deadlines systematically. Furthermore, a single Unified Admin Dashboard provides role-governed data views for University Scholarship Office staff, interview panels, and school subcommittees, maintaining a human decision-maker at every critical evaluation point. The third architectural contribution of the platform is a proactive, event-driven notification subsystem that delivers automated SMS and email alerts via Iprogsms and Resend, triggered both by deadline proximity and by application status mutations, ensuring that students receive time-critical updates without relying on passive communication channels that have historically contributed to missed deadlines and unutilized funding at the institutional level.

\section{Problem Statement}

Ateneo de Davao University (AdDU) lacks a digital system to manage the admission process of its internal scholarship tracks. The current reliance on a paper-based procedure creates systemic strain on applicants and on the offices and committees that evaluate them. Specifically, the current process presents the following problems:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single-Phase, Paper-Heavy Document Collection:} Applicants must assemble and physically submit a complete file at once. A missing or illegible document can lead to an abrupt rejection, with no structured window for correction.
  \item \textbf{Fragmented Committee Data and Untraceable Decisions:} Five School Scholarship Subcommittees apply different selection criteria to approximately 1,326 applicants per cycle, and their data must be collated manually. Decisions are not recorded in a form that traces each status change to the person who made it.
  \item \textbf{Status and Deadline Communication Deficits:} The absence of proactive status updates and deadline reminders leaves applicants unaware of submission windows and correction requests, leading to missed deadlines and incomplete files.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Specific Research Questions}

Consequently, this study seeks to answer the following specific research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item What is the perceived reduction in the time and effort AdDU scholarship applicants spend on the application process when using the two-stage digital submission of ScholarPath AdDU compared to the current paper-based process?
  \item How does the ScholarPath AdDU Application Lifecycle workflow perform in terms of (a) functional correctness, specifically whether status transitions, including Conditional Revert and Lapsed, match the expected outputs of a structured Lifecycle Test Case Matrix with no status change lacking a recorded human actor, and (b) perceived usefulness of the Unified Admin Dashboard to University Scholarship Office staff and School Scholarship Subcommittee members?
  \item To what extent does the automated notification subsystem reduce missed submission deadlines and uncorrected incomplete files among applicants?
  \item What is the overall system usability and performance efficiency of ScholarPath AdDU as evaluated by users based on the ISO/IEC 25010 functional suitability standards?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Objectives of the Study}

\textbf{General Objective:} To design, develop, and evaluate ScholarPath AdDU, a web-based centralized scholarship discovery and application tracking system for Ateneo de Davao University students. The platform aims to replace manual application processes with a digital system that provides centralized access to financial aid information.

\textbf{Specific Objectives:}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item To develop a first-pass eligibility screening engine, the Smart Eligibility Checker, that evaluates an applicant's high school average, entrance exam result, household income, and chosen degree program against the requirements of the selected internal scholarship track, applying a priority-based Exclusion Flag Hierarchy to resolve policy contradictions and flag each application for admin review before it proceeds.
  \item To design a centralized digital repository that integrates AdDU's three internal scholarship tracks (Jubilee, Grant-in-Aid, and Working Scholars) and their applicant records within a unified, searchable platform supported by compound-indexed dynamic faceted search.
  \item To implement a proactive notification and tracking system that alerts students to application statuses and strictly enforced deadlines through automated, event-driven SMS and email delivery.
  \item To administer a Pre-Study Student Problem Validation Survey among current or former AdDU scholarship applicants to establish baseline data on the perceived time and effort burden of the existing manual process, and to administer a Post-Prototype Perceived Effort Survey to the same cohort following prototype testing, enabling a direct comparative analysis of perceived effort reduction across Research Questions 1 through 3.
  \item To evaluate the ScholarPath AdDU functional prototype using a task-based usability testing protocol aligned with ISO/IEC 25010 standards, measuring functional suitability, performance efficiency, and system usability via the System Usability Scale (SUS).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Significance of the Study}

The implementation of ScholarPath AdDU provides practical, structural benefits to key stakeholders within the university ecosystem. For students, the system offers a streamlined, digital gateway that consolidates financial aid discovery and eliminates the need for manual, physical document submissions. By automating eligibility matching, the platform ensures equitable access to information, helping to mitigate the discovery friction that prevents students from securing funding. For University Scholarship Office staff, interview panels, school subcommittees, and the Scholarship Director, the platform replaces separate paper routing with a single Unified Admin Dashboard, through which each evaluator verifies documents, records ratings, or endorses decisions within a role-scoped view. This reduces the operational bottlenecks caused by managing fragmented records across three internal scholarship tracks, enabling staff to allocate more time to higher-value advisory functions.

Additionally, this study contributes to the broader field of higher education management by providing a documented architectural framework for migrating from paper-based to fully digital scholarship systems. It serves as a replicable model for similar institutions managing multi-track financial aid portfolios under the constraints of national data privacy legislation.

\section{Scope and Limitations}

\textbf{Scope}

This study is bounded by the design, development, and evaluation of a functional web application prototype for Ateneo de Davao University. The platform focuses on the digital application processing and committee decision support for incoming Grade 12 applicants across AdDU's three internal scholarship tracks: Jubilee, Grant-in-Aid, and Working Scholars. It encompasses first-pass eligibility screening, two-stage digital document submissions, administrative verification, and committee evaluation workflows. External scholarship grants and monetary disbursements are outside the scope of selection and processing.

The system scope includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Target Audience Coverage:} The platform is designed for incoming college applicants, University Scholarship Office staff, interview panel members, and the five school subcommittees (SAS, SBG, SEA, SON, SOE).
  \item \textbf{Information Coverage:} The repository consolidates applicant files, credential verification statuses, and evaluation metrics for the three internal scholarship tracks.
  \item \textbf{Core Functionality:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item An automated algorithm that evaluates student profiles (Quality Point Index, household income, and enrolled degree program) to provide personalized scholarship matching.
    \item A secure digital repository allowing students to upload necessary prerequisites (e.g., academic transcripts, BIR-stamped ITRs) once to attach to multiple applications, eliminating the need for manual physical submissions.
    \item An automated notification subsystem delivering critical deadline alerts and status updates via SMS and email.
    \item A Unified Admin Dashboard through which Scholarship Staff broadcast announcements, monitor pending applications, and track real-time analytics, and through which School Subcommittees filter and assess applicants within their school, including household income for Grant-in-Aid (GIA) selections. All evaluator roles work in this single interface; each role's view is scoped by Row Level Security (Table~\ref{tab:rbac}).
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Development Approach:} The prototype implements a hybrid mobile and web architecture to ensure cross-platform compatibility. The system utilizes standard web frameworks and cloud-based databases to manage real-time synchronization, cloud storage, and secure user authentication.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation Scope:} Usability testing based on ISO/IEC 25010 standards will involve a purposive sample of approximately 10 participants, comprising AdDU scholarship applicants, University Scholarship Office staff, and selected School Scholarship Subcommittee members. The evaluation will use a mixed-method approach, combining task-based observational testing (measuring completion rates and time-on-task) with two sequential post-session instruments: the System Usability Scale (SUS) to assess perceived interface usability, and the Post-Prototype Perceived Effort Survey (Appendix C) to generate comparative data against the pre-study baseline from Appendix B. Results will be quantified using descriptive statistics to measure the perceived reduction in processing times.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations}

This study acknowledges the following realistic constraints that define its boundaries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Financial Transaction Limitation:} The prototype functions solely as a discovery, matching, and application-tracking platform. It does not handle actual monetary disbursements, bank routing, or direct tuition fee deductions from the university's finance ledger.
  \item \textbf{Administrative Access Constraints:} The system centralizes information regarding external scholarships but does not grant external organizations (e.g., DOST, SM Foundation) direct administrative access to the AdDU database.
  \item \textbf{External Submission Dependencies:} For specific external grants, the system acts only as an information and tracking hub. Students may still be mandated to complete final submissions or examinations through official third-party portals, such as the DOST E-Scholarship Application System.
  \item \textbf{Predictive Matching Only:} The automated matching engine predicts eligibility based on the input data; it does not guarantee a grant. The final evaluation, interview assessments, and approval of financial aid remain under the strict, manual jurisdiction of the University Scholarship Office, the Interview Panel, the School Scholarship Subcommittees, the Scholarship Director, or the external granting agencies.
  \item \textbf{Decision Support Only:} The system serves strictly as a decision-support platform. No module selects, ranks, defers, or awards scholarships automatically; all final selection decisions remain under the exclusive jurisdiction of human evaluators and school subcommittees.
  \item \textbf{Credential Verification Boundary:} High school averages and uploaded documents are verified 100\% inside the University Scholarship Office against official submitted report cards, with no third-party or external database verifier.
  \item \textbf{User Base Limitation:} System access is restricted to AdDU scholarship applicants and authorized university personnel. This includes incoming Grade 12 applicants as well as officially enrolled college students. Other basic education units (Grade School, Junior High, Senior High) outside the scholarship applicant pool, Graduate School students, and the general public are excluded from this platform.
  \item \textbf{Testing Scope Limitation:} Usability evaluation involves a purposive sample of participants rather than a university-wide deployment. Findings will inform prototype refinement for the capstone requirements but do not constitute definitive statistical claims about the system's efficacy across the entire AdDU population.
  \item \textbf{Prototype-Based Time Estimation Constraint:} The participant time estimates collected through the Post-Prototype Perceived Effort Survey (Appendix C) are based on a single, controlled prototype testing session rather than sustained real-world use of the deployed system. Consequently, these estimates may not fully reflect actual time-on-process in practice, as factors such as varying network conditions, individual document readiness, and accumulated platform familiarity over repeated use could produce meaningfully different results from those observed during the evaluation session.
\end{itemize}
