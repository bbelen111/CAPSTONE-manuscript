\section{Background of the Study}

Globally, universities are transitioning from traditional, paper-based administrative workflows to digital ecosystems to enhance student access to educational funding. This digitalization is increasingly necessary due to rising educational costs and expanding student populations. In the Philippines, the steady growth of the higher education sector \cite{ref29} has coincided with tuition fee increases across numerous private institutions, driven by inflation and operational adjustments \cite{ref11,ref19}. While financial aid is available through state-sponsored grants \cite{ref21,ref44}, international educational investments \cite{ref45}, and private corporate foundations \cite{ref1,ref40}, discovering and accessing these opportunities remains a challenge due to decentralized information. At Ateneo de Davao University (AdDU), students face similar financial pressures, exacerbated by recent and proposed tuition increases. To support its student body, AdDU manages a complex financial aid ecosystem comprising 54 distinct funding pipelines, which include internally funded endowments, external corporate partnerships, and state-sponsored grants \cite{ref2,ref8}. However, the application process for these diverse avenues remains largely traditional and decentralized. Students are required to navigate fragmented information sources and submit physical documentation, such as Form 230-SCH and sealed recommendation letters. This reliance on manual documentation creates severe operational bottlenecks for the Office of Student Affairs (OSA) and places an administrative burden on applicants, frequently resulting in eligible students remaining unaware of assistance and scholarship funds being left unutilized. To address this, this project proposes ScholarPath AdDU --- a centralized web-based platform whose primary technical contribution is a rule-based automated eligibility matching engine, the Smart Eligibility Checker, which cross-references a student's Quality Point Index (QPI), household income, and enrolled degree program against the specific, intersecting constraints of all 54 grants simultaneously. Unlike existing scholarship portals in the Philippine higher education literature, which stop at information dissemination or submission routing, the Smart Eligibility Checker employs a forward-chaining inference engine with an Exclusion Flag Hierarchy --- a conflict resolution mechanism that ensures specific policy exclusions unconditionally override general eligibility criteria before any grant is surfaced to a student's dashboard. This removes the burden of manual self-assessment that prior systems \cite{ref14} have identified as the primary driver of erroneous and mismatched scholarship applications. The platform's second core innovation, the Document Vault, employs a metadata-driven, one-time-upload architecture backed by Supabase Storage, allowing students to attach the same verified document to multiple applications without re-submission and enabling OSA administrators to validate documents through a unified, role-governed dashboard. The third architectural contribution of the platform is a proactive, event-driven notification subsystem that delivers automated SMS and email alerts via Twilio and SendGrid, triggered both by deadline proximity and by application status mutations --- ensuring that students receive time-critical updates without relying on passive communication channels that have historically contributed to missed deadlines and unutilized funding at the institutional level.

\section{Problem Statement}

Ateneo de Davao University (AdDU) lacks a centralized digital ecosystem to manage its financial aid landscape of over 50 distinct programs. The university's current reliance on outdated, manual application processes creates a systemic breakdown in the scholarship pipeline, which is particularly urgent given recent tuition fee increases. Specifically, the current system presents the following problems: 1. Information Fragmentation and Discovery Friction: Scholarship details are scattered across 54 distinct internal and external pipelines. Because information is decentralized, students struggle to identify eligible funding and must manually align internal university processing deadlines with unpredictable external corporate timelines. 2. Inefficient Processing and Matching Errors: The requirement for physical document submission (e.g., Form 230-SCH) causes severe operational delays at the OSA. Additionally, the lack of automated matching requires students to manually navigate complex eligibility criteria, frequently resulting in application errors and mismatched submissions. 3. Tracking and Notification Deficits: The absence of proactive, automated status updates and notifications leads to missed application deadlines, disqualifying students from securing necessary financial aid. Specific Research Questions Consequently, this study seeks to answer the following specific questions: 1. What is the perceived reduction in the time AdDU students spend discovering and applying for financial aid when using the centralized ScholarPath AdDU repository compared to the current manual process? 2. How does the Smart Eligibility Checker perform in terms of (a) functional correctness --- specifically, whether the engine's Exclusion Flag Hierarchy accurately produces expected outputs across edge-case student profiles as evaluated through a structured test case matrix --- and (b) perceived matching effectiveness, as reported by student participants comparing their prior experience of manual eligibility self-assessment against their post-prototype perception of automated eligibility determination? 3. To what extent does the automated notification subsystem reduce the incidence of missed application deadlines among AdDU students? 4. What is the overall system usability and performance efficiency of ScholarPath AdDU as evaluated by users based on the ISO/IEC 25010 functional suitability standards?

\section{Objectives of the Study}

General Objective: To design, develop, and evaluate ScholarPath AdDU, a web-based centralized scholarship discovery and application tracking system for Ateneo de Davao University students. The platform aims to replace manual application processes with a digital system that provides centralized access to financial aid information. Specific Objectives: 1. To develop an automated eligibility matching engine --- the Smart Eligibility Checker --- that evaluates a student's QPI, household income, and enrolled degree program against the intersecting constraints of all available financial aid programs, applying a priority-based Exclusion Flag Hierarchy to resolve policy contradictions and surface only verifiably eligible grants to the student's dashboard. 2. To design a centralized digital repository that integrates AdDU's portfolio of over 50 distinct scholarship programs --- including internal grants, external corporate partnerships such as Davao Light and SM Foundation, and state-sponsored grants such as CHED and DOST --- within a unified, searchable platform supported by compound-indexed dynamic faceted search. 3. To implement a proactive notification and tracking system that alerts students to application statuses and strictly enforced deadlines through automated, event-driven SMS and email delivery. 4. To administer a Pre-Study Student Problem Validation Survey among current or former AdDU scholarship applicants to establish baseline data on the perceived time and effort burden of the existing manual process, and to administer a Post-Prototype Perceived Effort Survey to the same cohort following prototype testing, enabling a direct comparative analysis of perceived effort reduction across Research Questions 1 through 3. 5. To evaluate the ScholarPath AdDU functional prototype using a task-based usability testing protocol aligned with ISO/IEC 25010 standards, measuring functional suitability, performance efficiency, and system usability via the System Usability Scale (SUS).

\section{Significance of the Study}

The implementation of ScholarPath AdDU provides practical, structural benefits to key stakeholders within the university ecosystem. For students, the system offers a streamlined, digital gateway that consolidates financial aid discovery and eliminates the need for manual, physical document submissions. By automating eligibility matching, the platform ensures equitable access to information, helping to mitigate the discovery friction that prevents students from securing funding. For the Office of Student Affairs (OSA), the platform digitizes application tracking, allowing administrators to verify documents and process approvals through a unified dashboard. This reduces the operational bottlenecks caused by managing fragmented records across 54 funding pipelines, enabling staff to allocate more time to higher-value advisory functions. Additionally, this study contributes to the broader field of higher education management by providing a documented architectural framework for migrating from paper-based to fully digital scholarship systems. It serves as a replicable model for similar institutions managing multi-pipeline financial aid portfolios under the constraints of national data privacy legislation.

\section{Scope and Limitations}

Scope This study is bounded by the design, development, and evaluation of a functional web and mobile application prototype for Ateneo de Davao University. The platform's scope is defined by its target user base, the range of financial aid information it consolidates, and the specific administrative functions it digitizes. It does not extend to actual monetary disbursement, integration with the university's finance ledger, or the granting of direct database access to external scholarship organizations. Within these boundaries, the system addresses the full digital lifecycle of scholarship discovery and application tracking --- from initial student profile registration and automated eligibility matching through to document submission, deadline notification, and OSA-level application processing. The system scope includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Target Audience Coverage: The platform is designed exclusively for the undergraduate college-level student body, authorized administrators within the AdDU Office of Student Affairs (OSA), and designated academic administrators, specifically Department Chairs and Coordinators.
  \item Information Coverage: The centralized repository integrates eligibility criteria, deadline schedules, and benefits for over 50 financial aid programs. This includes government grants (e.g., CHED, DOST), corporate endowments (e.g., Aboitiz, SM Foundation), and AdDU internal programs (e.g., Grant-in-Aid, Working Student grants).
  \item Core Functionality:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item An automated algorithm that evaluates student profiles (Quality Point Index, household income, and enrolled degree program) to provide personalized scholarship matching.
    \item A secure digital repository allowing students to upload necessary prerequisites (e.g., academic transcripts, BIR-stamped ITRs) once to attach to multiple applications, eliminating the need for manual physical submissions.
    \item An automated notification subsystem delivering critical deadline alerts and status updates via SMS and email.
    \item A backend Content Management System (CMS) enabling OSA administrators to broadcast announcements, monitor pending requests, and track real-time analytics. Additionally, the system features a dedicated review interface for Department Chairs and Coordinators to efficiently filter and assess applicant economic statuses for Grant-in-Aid (GIA) selections.
  \end{itemize}
  \item Development Approach: The prototype implements a hybrid mobile and web architecture to ensure cross-platform compatibility. The system utilizes standard web frameworks and cloud-based databases to manage real-time synchronization, cloud storage, and secure user authentication.
  \item Evaluation Scope: Usability testing based on ISO/IEC 25010 standards will involve a purposive sample of approximately 10 to 15 AdDU college students, active OSA administrative staff, and selected Department Chairs or Coordinators. The evaluation will use a mixed-method approach, combining task-based observational testing (measuring completion rates and time-on-task) with two sequential post-session instruments: the System Usability Scale (SUS) to assess perceived interface usability, and the Post-Prototype Perceived Effort Survey (Appendix C) to generate comparative data against the pre-study baseline from Appendix B. Results will be quantified using descriptive statistics to measure the perceived reduction in processing times. Limitations This study acknowledges the following realistic constraints that define its boundaries:
  \item Financial Transaction Limitation: The prototype functions solely as a discovery, matching, and application-tracking platform. It does not handle actual monetary disbursements, bank routing, or direct tuition fee deductions from the university's finance ledger.
  \item Administrative Access Constraints: The system centralizes information regarding external scholarships but does not grant external organizations (e.g., DOST, SM Foundation) direct administrative access to the AdDU database.
  \item External Submission Dependencies: For specific external grants, the system acts only as an information and tracking hub. Students may still be mandated to complete final submissions or examinations through official third-party portals, such as the DOST E-Scholarship Application System.
  \item Predictive Matching Only: The automated matching engine predicts eligibility based on the input data; it does not guarantee a grant. The final evaluation, interview assessments, and approval of financial aid remain under the strict, manual jurisdiction of the AdDU College Scholarship Committee, Department Chairs and Coordinators (for GIA), or the external granting agencies.
  \item Registrar Integration Limitation: The platform does not integrate with the AdDU University Registrar's information system. Student QPI data is self-reported by the student during account registration and is stored exclusively within ScholarPath AdDU's PostgreSQL database. This data is not cross-referenced against or validated against the Registrar's official academic records prior to eligibility matching. Consequently, the accuracy of eligibility determinations is contingent on the honesty and accuracy of the student's self-reported academic standing.
  \item Budget Allocation Constraint: The platform does not implement an algorithm for determining which among multiple qualified applicants should receive a scholarship award when the number of eligible applicants exceeds the number of available funded slots for a given grant. The system's automated matching engine identifies and surfaces all students who meet a scholarship's eligibility criteria; however, the final awarding decision --- including any priority ranking or selection among equally qualified applicants --- remains under the exclusive jurisdiction of the AdDU College Scholarship Committee, the relevant Department Chairs and Coordinators (for Grant-in-Aid selections), or the respective external granting agencies. A future system enhancement could explore weighted scoring or preference-based allocation algorithms to assist administrators in this selection phase, but such functionality is explicitly outside the scope of the present prototype.
  \item User Base Limitation: System access is restricted exclusively to bona fide, officially enrolled AdDU college undergraduate students and specific university personnel. Basic education units (Grade School, Junior High, Senior High), Graduate School students, and the general public are excluded from this platform.
  \item Testing Scope Limitation: Usability evaluation involves a purposive sample of participants rather than a university-wide deployment. Findings will inform prototype refinement for the capstone requirements but do not constitute definitive statistical claims about the system's efficacy across the entire AdDU population.
  \item Prototype-Based Time Estimation Constraint: The participant time estimates collected through the Post-Prototype Perceived Effort Survey (Appendix C) are based on a single, controlled prototype testing session rather than sustained real-world use of the deployed system. Consequently, these estimates may not fully reflect actual time-on-process in practice, as factors such as varying network conditions, individual document readiness, and accumulated platform familiarity over repeated use could produce meaningfully different results from those observed during the evaluation session.
\end{itemize}
